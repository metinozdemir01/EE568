1. Introduction

This report outlines the primary objectives and scope of the torque analysis for a variable reluctance machine. The study encompasses analytical derivations and computational verifications using COMSOL Multiphysics. Both analytical models and Finite Element Analysis (FEA) are employed to evaluate the magnetic circuit under linear and non-linear material conditions. Finally, a practical on-off control method is proposed to sustain continuous rotation based on the obtained torque profiles.

2. Analytical Modelling (Q1)

2.1 Reluctance, Inductance and Torque Calculations (Q1-a, Q1-b)

The maximum and minimum reluctance values of the variable reluctance machine were analytically calculated under the assumption that the core is infinitely permeable. This assumption simplifies the magnetic circuit to consist primarily of the air-gap reluctances. Based on the geometric dimensions provided, the maximum inductance at the aligned position (\(L_{max}\)) was calculated as 0.0424 H, and the minimum inductance at the fully unaligned position (\(L_{min}\)) was 0.00848 H. The inductance variation is assumed to follow a continuous sinusoidal profile as a function of the rotor angle \(\theta\). The mathematical expression for the inductance is given by:

\[ L(\theta) = L_0 + L_2 \cos(2\theta) \]

where the average inductance \(L_0\) and the inductance amplitude \(L_2\) are defined as:

\[ L_0 = \frac{L_{max} + L_{min}}{2} \quad , \quad L_2 = \frac{L_{max} - L_{min}}{2} \]

Under a constant DC excitation current \(I\) of 2.5 A, the torque generated by the system is derived from the spatial derivative of the magnetic co-energy \(W_c\). The co-energy in a linear magnetic system is expressed as:

\[ W_c = \frac{1}{2} L(\theta) I^2 \]

The electromagnetic torque \(T_e\) is obtained by differentiating the co-energy with respect to the rotor position \(\theta\):

\[ T_e = \frac{\partial W_c}{\partial \theta} = -I^2 L_2 \sin(2\theta) \]

Using the calculated values, the analytical torque function demonstrates a theoretical peak value of 106 mN·m.

2.2 Method to Include Non-linear Effects (Q1-c)

To improve the analytical model, non-linear effects such as fringing flux and non-homogeneous flux distribution must be considered. Fringing at the air gaps increases the effective cross-sectional area, which effectively decreases the total air-gap reluctance and alters the inductance curve. Carter's coefficient can be utilized to mathematically account for these fringing fields in analytical equations without performing full finite element analysis.

3.1 Flux Density Vectors (Q2-a)

The variable reluctance machine was modeled in COMSOL Multiphysics using a linear soft iron material with a constant relative permeability (\(\mu_r\)) of 2500. The flux density vectors for the rotor positioned at 0 degrees, 45 degrees, and 90 degrees were evaluated. Figure 1 and Figure 2 present the aligned and partially unaligned positions, respectively. In this unaligned state, the reluctance of the magnetic circuit reaches its maximum due to the large effective air gap, resulting in a significantly lower and dispersed magnetic flux density across the core compared to the fully aligned position.

Unlike the linear analysis, a time-dependent dynamic study was performed on the non-linear model to accurately capture the system's response. At the fully aligned position (0 degrees), the stored energy was calculated as 0.135 J with an inductance of 0.0433 H. 4.4 Effects of Fringing and Saturation (Q3-d)

Comparing the analytical, linear, and non-linear results highlights the compounding impacts of fringing fields and magnetic saturation on torque production. The reluctance torque is fundamentally driven by the rate of change of inductance concerning position. Fringing fields, which occur when flux leaves the high-permeability iron path to bulge through the surrounding air, are most dominant at unaligned positions (e.g., 90 degrees). This phenomenon effectively increases the cross-sectional area of the air gap, raising the minimum inductance limit compared to idealized models.

Conversely, saturation occurs predominantly at aligned positions (0 and 180 degrees) where the magnetic flux is highly concentrated. The non-linear soft iron struggles to accommodate the increasing flux density, leading to a sharp drop in relative permeability. To illustrate this, local flux density measurements reveal that while the idealized linear model permits extreme concentrations up to 2.36 T at the sharp inner corners and 1.06 T at the core center, the physical non-linear model caps these values at 1.4 T and 0.97 T, respectively. This saturation effect heavily restricts the maximum achievable inductance. Because torque production relies on maximizing the difference between aligned and unaligned inductances, the simultaneous increase of minimum inductance due to fringing and the suppression of maximum inductance due to saturation severely compresses the actual torque output of the physical machine.

5. Control Method (Q4)

To achieve continuous rotation, a position-dependent On/Off DC current excitation method is proposed. The torque production principle of a variable reluctance machine dictates that positive torque is generated only when the rotor is moving from an unaligned position towards an aligned position, corresponding to regions where inductance is increasing. The extracted data confirms negative torque between 0 and 90 degrees, and positive torque between 90 and 180 degrees. Therefore, a rotor position sensor can be utilized to supply the 2.5 A DC current solely when the rotor is positioned between 90-180 degrees and 270-360 degrees. By completely cutting off the excitation current during the intervals where the inductance is decreasing, the generation of negative torque is effectively prevented, ensuring a strictly positive average torque and stable continuous rotation.

7. Conclusion

This project successfully modeled and analyzed the torque characteristics of a variable reluctance machine. The transition from a simplified analytical approach to a linear simulation, and finally to a non-linear time-dependent dynamic analysis, demonstrated the critical importance of physical factors. The comparison graphs and evaluation data conclusively proved that analytical models overestimate performance, while non-linear FEA provides a realistic operational baseline by correctly interpreting the restrictive impacts of magnetic saturation and fringing flux. Furthermore, the proposed position-dependent On/Off control method verified the theoretical requirements for practical, continuous positive torque production.
